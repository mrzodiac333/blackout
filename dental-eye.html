Dental & Eye Care

Manage toothache, dental trauma and eye injuries when a dentist or ophthalmologist is not available.

Medical Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read

Dental and eye problems are rarely fatal but can become disabling. The goal is to reduce pain, prevent infection and preserve the tooth or eye until professional care is possible.

This is not medical training

Read this to recognise emergencies and act until help arrives — not to replace a certified course. Take a Red Cross or St John Ambulance first-aid and CPR class. Never perform procedures you have not been trained for.

Toothache and dental infection

  • Rinse with warm salt water; floss gently to remove trapped food.
  • Apply clove oil on a cotton pledget near the tooth for temporary relief.
  • Use over-the-counter pain relief per the label.
  • Cold compress for swelling; do not apply heat to a swollen face.
  • See a dentist as soon as possible. Facial swelling, fever or difficulty swallowing or breathing is an emergency — go to hospital.

Knocked-out tooth

  1. Handle the tooth by the crown, not the root.
  2. Rinse gently with saline or milk; do not scrub.
  3. If an adult tooth, reinsert into the socket and bite gently on gauze; if you cannot, store it in milk or saline.
  4. Get to a dentist within 30–60 minutes.
  5. Do not reinsert baby teeth.

Eye injuries

  • Chemical splash: rinse with clean water or saline for at least 15–20 minutes, from the inner corner outward, and seek urgent care. Remove contact lenses if present.
  • Foreign object: do not rub. Try flushing; if the object is embedded or on the cornea, do not remove it — cover both eyes lightly and seek care.
  • Blunt trauma: cold compress, do not press on the eye, seek care.
  • Penetrating injury: do not remove the object; cover with a rigid shield (a paper cup) and go to hospital immediately.

Prevention and hygiene

  • Wear safety glasses when cutting, drilling or handling chemicals.
  • Do not share towels or eye drops.
  • Wash hands before touching your eyes.
  • Replace toothbrushes after illness; store upright and separate.
  • Limit sugary snacks between meals; rinse with water after eating when brushing is impossible.
