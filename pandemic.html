Scenario: Pandemic

Reduce exposure, care for the sick at home, and keep a household running through an infectious outbreak.

Scenarios Last reviewed 2026-10-19 8 min read

Pandemics are slow emergencies. The goal is to reduce contacts, protect the vulnerable and keep essential care going without overwhelming hospitals.

Reducing exposure

  • Limit contacts; work from home if possible.
  • Wear a well-fitted N95/FFP2 mask in crowded indoor spaces.
  • Wash hands frequently; avoid touching your face.
  • Ventilate rooms; meet outdoors when possible.
  • Isolate early if symptomatic; test if available.
  • Protect high-risk household members by separating sleeping and eating spaces.

Caring for someone at home

  • Designate one caregiver and one room; keep the door closed.
  • The caregiver wears a mask and gloves; washes hands after contact.
  • Use separate utensils, towels and bedding; wash at high temperature.
  • Monitor temperature, breathing and hydration; keep a written log.
  • Give fluids and rest; use paracetamol per label for fever.
  • Clean high-touch surfaces daily with detergent or diluted bleach.
  • Dispose of waste in a tied bag, then another bag.
Seek urgent care

Difficulty breathing, chest pain, confusion, bluish lips, persistent high fever, or inability to keep fluids down. Call 112 or the local health helpline.

Supplies and logistics

  • Two weeks of food, water and medicine minimum; four weeks is better.
  • Thermometer, pulse oximeter if available, ORS, paracetamol, masks, gloves.
  • Plan for medicine refills and teleconsultations.
  • Arrange contactless delivery or a neighbour to drop supplies.
  • Keep cash and a list of emergency contacts.

After the wave

  • Rest and recover fully; watch for lingering symptoms.
  • Replace used supplies and medicines.
  • Check on neighbours and vulnerable people.
  • Review what you would do differently.
