Emergency Childbirth: Immediate Support

What to do if birth is imminent before help arrives. Support and safety only — not a substitute for maternity care.

Medical Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read
Call for help first

Call 112 or your local emergency number immediately. This page covers only immediate support when birth is imminent and help cannot arrive in time. It is not a childbirth course. Complications require professional care.

Birth can progress faster than expected, especially in a second or later pregnancy. Prepare a clean space, keep the mother warm and calm, and do not pull on the baby.

Signs birth is imminent

  • Regular contractions less than 2 minutes apart
  • Strong urge to push
  • Bulging or visible baby's head at the vaginal opening
  • Waters broken with strong contractions

If any of these are present, do not try to travel far; call for help and prepare to support birth where you are.

Supporting the birth

  1. Wash hands and wear gloves if available. Prepare clean towels and a warm, dry space.
  2. Help the mother into a comfortable position; encourage slow breathing and pushing with contractions.
  3. As the head appears, support it gently with a clean cloth. Do not pull.
  4. After the head delivers, check whether the cord is around the neck; if so, gently slip it over the head.
  5. Support the body as it delivers; the baby will be wet and slippery.
  6. Dry the baby immediately with a clean towel, cover the head, and place skin-to-skin on the mother's chest.
  7. Note the time of birth. Do not cut the cord unless trained and the baby is stable; if you must, use a sterile tie and clean blade.
  8. Keep the mother and baby warm; encourage breastfeeding if both are well.

When to get urgent help

  • Heavy bleeding after birth
  • Baby not breathing or not crying after drying and stimulation
  • Cord prolapse (cord comes out before the baby)
  • Baby's arm or foot appears first
  • Labour lasting many hours with no progress
  • Mother confused, pale, or with a seizure
  • Fever or foul-smelling discharge

For a baby not breathing: call for help, dry and stimulate, and begin newborn resuscitation if trained. Keep the baby warm — hypothermia kills newborns quickly.

After birth

  • Keep mother and baby warm and together; skin-to-skin regulates the baby's temperature.
  • Monitor bleeding; massage the lower abdomen if trained and bleeding is heavy.
  • Give fluids and food to the mother; rest is essential.
  • Watch for infection signs in both mother and baby.
  • Get professional postnatal care as soon as possible — check the baby, the mother and feeding.
