Mental Health in Crisis

Understand stress reactions, support people through loss, protect sleep, and know when to seek professional help.

Medical Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

Disasters produce fear, anger, grief and exhaustion. These are normal responses to abnormal events. What protects people is connection, routine, information and rest — not toughness.

Common stress reactions

  • Anxiety, irritability, anger, numbness
  • Difficulty sleeping, nightmares, fatigue
  • Poor concentration and memory
  • Headaches, stomach upset, appetite changes
  • Withdrawal from others
  • Increased alcohol or substance use

Most reactions ease over days to weeks with support. They are not weakness.

Supporting others

  • Be present. Listen without forcing someone to talk.
  • Practical help — water, food, a phone call, childcare — matters more than advice.
  • Keep information honest and age-appropriate, especially with children.
  • Reunite families as soon as possible; separation is a major stressor.
  • Watch for people at higher risk: those bereaved, injured, isolated, or with prior mental illness.
  • Do not tell people to "just be strong" or compare their suffering to others'.

Children and adolescents

  • Give simple, honest explanations; avoid graphic details.
  • Maintain routines — meals, bedtime, schoolwork, play.
  • Allow extra closeness and reassurance.
  • Limit media exposure to the disaster.
  • Watch for regression (bedwetting, clinginess), aggression, sleep problems or withdrawal; seek help if persistent.

When to get professional help

Seek help promptly if someone:

  • Talks about suicide or self-harm
  • Cannot function for more than two weeks
  • Stops eating or sleeping for days
  • Shows severe agitation or confusion
  • Increases alcohol or drug use
  • Experiences hallucinations or paranoia

India: Tele-MANAS 14416 (24×7, free). In immediate danger, call 112. Never leave a person at risk of suicide alone; remove access to means and stay with them.

Small things that help

Sleep, water, food, movement, sunlight, a routine, and one person who listens. Repeat them daily.
