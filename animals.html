Animals, Snakebite & Pests

Snakebite and rabies first aid, pet and livestock care in emergencies, and keeping pests out of your supplies.

Survival Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

India has some of the world's highest snakebite and rabies burdens. Most encounters are avoidable. Know the first aid that helps and the myths that harm.

Snakebite

Do this — and only this

Call 112 or get to a hospital immediately. Keep the person still and calm, immobilise the bitten limb at or below heart level, remove rings and tight items, and note the time of the bite. Do not cut, suck, burn, tourniquet or apply ice.

  • Most Indian bites occur at night, on the lower leg, in rural areas and during monsoon.
  • Use a torch, wear closed shoes, and shake out bedding and footwear before use.
  • Do not try to catch or kill the snake; a photo from a safe distance helps treatment.
  • Antivenom is the only effective treatment and is given in hospital.
  • Watch for swelling, bleeding, drooping eyelids, difficulty breathing or paralysis — these are emergencies.

Animal bites and rabies

  • Wash the wound immediately with soap and running water for 15 minutes.
  • Apply an antiseptic (povidone-iodine) and cover loosely.
  • Go to a hospital or anti-rabies clinic the same day. Rabies is fatal once symptoms start, but post-exposure vaccination is highly effective if started promptly.
  • Do not apply chilli, ash, turmeric or herbal pastes to the wound.
  • Note the animal, its behaviour, and whether it is vaccinated; report stray bites to local authorities.

Pets in emergencies

  • Prepare a pet go-bag: 7 days of food, water, bowls, leash, carrier, medications, vaccination record and a photo.
  • Keep pets indoors during storms and civil unrest; never leave them tied outside.
  • Microchip and tag pets; keep a current photo on your phone.
  • Identify pet-friendly shelters or boarding before you need them.
  • Comfort animals during displacement — they show stress through hiding, pacing and refusal to eat.

Pests and disease vectors

  • Remove standing water to control mosquitoes; use nets and repellent.
  • Keep grains and dry food in sealed metal or thick plastic containers.
  • Set traps rather than poison where children and pets are present.
  • Wear gloves when handling dead animals and disinfect afterwards.
  • Rats and cockroaches signal sanitation problems — clean, seal entry points and remove food sources.
