Fitness, Morale & Daily Life

Stay physically capable and mentally steady through a prolonged disruption: movement, sleep, play and purpose.

Skills & Security Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read

Endurance is a survival skill. Fitness makes evacuation, carrying water and recovery easier; morale keeps a household functioning. Both are built before the emergency.

Fitness for real tasks

  • Walking: build to 5–10 km with a loaded backpack.
  • Carrying: practise lifting and carrying water containers safely.
  • Strength: squats, push-ups, rows and core work; bodyweight is enough.
  • Mobility: stretch hips, shoulders and back daily.
  • Swimming: a life-saving skill in flood-prone regions.
  • Train in heat and cold gradually; hydrate and pace.

Sleep and recovery

  • Protect 7–9 hours; sleep debt impairs judgement.
  • Keep a consistent bedtime even in a shelter.
  • Use earplugs and an eye mask; darkness matters.
  • Avoid caffeine late in the day and alcohol as a sleep aid.
  • Nap strategically if night duties are shared.

Morale and routine

  • Keep a daily rhythm: wake, meals, chores, rest, check-in, wind down.
  • Give everyone a meaningful role — children included.
  • Celebrate small wins and mark birthdays and festivals.
  • Tell stories, play cards, listen to music, read aloud.
  • Limit doom-scrolling; set two information checks per day.
  • Laugh when you can. It genuinely helps.

Purpose and community

  • Helping others reduces helplessness and builds trust.
  • Share skills: teaching is one of the best ways to learn.
  • Plan for the future, even small plans — they signal hope.
  • Check on people living alone and include them.
