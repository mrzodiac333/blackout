Sanitation & Waste

Emergency toilets, waste handling and disease prevention when plumbing and collection stop working.

Survival Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

When toilets stop flushing, human waste becomes the fastest route to disease. Plan a latrine before the emergency, and keep it well away from water sources and food.

Emergency latrines

  • Distance: at least 30 m from wells and surface water, and downhill from them.
  • Depth: 1–1.2 m; cover each use with soil, ash or lime.
  • Privacy: a screen, curtain or tarp.
  • Hand-washing: soap and water immediately beside the exit path.
  • Never: site latrines in flood paths or near kitchens.

Bucket toilets indoors

For apartment dwellers and people sheltering in place:

  • Use a sturdy bucket with a fitted lid and a toilet seat if possible.
  • Line with a strong bag; add sawdust, ash or cat litter after each use to control smell and moisture.
  • Separate urine where possible — it can be diluted and used on non-edible plants.
  • Empty only into a designated disposal point, never into storm drains or open ground near water.
  • Clean the bucket with disinfectant after each emptying and wash hands.

Solid waste

  • Separate food waste, recyclables and hazardous waste (batteries, medical sharps).
  • Bag and tie waste; store away from living and food areas.
  • Bury food waste at least 30 m from water if collection has stopped.
  • Sharps go in a rigid container with a lid; never in loose bags.
  • Medical waste from caring for a sick person should be bagged separately and disposed of per local guidance.

Preventing outbreaks

  • Water, sanitation and hygiene are one system — protect all three together.
  • Watch for diarrhoea clusters; treat dehydration with ORS and seek care.
  • Isolate sick household members where possible and use separate utensils.
  • Disinfect surfaces and bedding; wash hands constantly.
  • Report unusual illness clusters to local health authorities.
