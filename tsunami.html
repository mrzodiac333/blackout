Tsunami

Coastal survival: recognise natural warnings, evacuate to high ground immediately, and stay away until the all-clear.

India Hazards Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read

India's coast is vulnerable to tsunamis from the Indian Ocean. Waves can arrive within minutes. Natural warnings are your best warning: strong shaking, a sudden sea retreat, or a loud roar.

Natural warning signs

  • Strong or prolonged earthquake shaking near the coast.
  • Sudden withdrawal of the sea, exposing the seabed.
  • A loud roar like a train or jet from the ocean.
  • Waves that look unusually large or behave strangely.
Do not wait

If you see or feel any of these, evacuate immediately to high ground or inland. Do not wait for an official warning.

Evacuation

  • Move to high ground — at least 30 m above sea level or 3 km inland if possible.
  • Use designated tsunami evacuation routes and signs.
  • Do not use vehicles if roads are congested; walk fast.
  • Stay away from rivers and estuaries; tsunamis travel up them.
  • Do not return until officials give the all-clear; waves come in sets.
  • If caught in water, grab something floating and let the current carry you; do not swim against it.

Preparing a coastal household

  • Know the evacuation zone and route; practise it.
  • Keep a go-bag by the door with documents, water, radio and medicines.
  • Know the local tsunami siren and alert system.
  • Keep the boat ready if you are a fisher, but do not go to sea on a tsunami warning.
  • Agree a family meeting point inland.

India-specific notes

  • The 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami hit Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Kerala and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands hard.
  • INCOIS issues tsunami bulletins; sirens are installed in some coastal villages.
  • The east coast is more exposed to Sunda Arc earthquakes; the west coast to Makran events.
  • Tsunami travel time to the Indian mainland can be 2–4 hours; to the Andamans, minutes.
