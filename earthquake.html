Scenario: Earthquake

Drop, cover and hold on — then deal with aftershocks, damaged buildings and trapped victims.

Scenarios Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

Earthquakes give little or no warning. The first seconds are about not being hit by falling objects. After shaking stops, the risks are aftershocks, gas leaks, fires and structural collapse.

During shaking

  1. Drop to hands and knees.
  2. Cover your head and neck under a sturdy table; if none, cover with your arms near an interior wall.
  3. Hold on until shaking stops.
  4. If in bed, stay there and cover your head with a pillow.
  5. If outdoors, move away from buildings, trees and power lines.
  6. If driving, pull over away from overpasses and stay in the vehicle.
Do not

Do not run outside during shaking, use lifts, or stand in doorways. Do not light matches after a quake — gas leaks are common.

After the shaking

  • Check yourself and others for injuries; treat bleeding first.
  • Expect aftershocks; drop, cover and hold on when they occur.
  • Shut off gas if you smell it; do not use electrical switches or open flames.
  • Check for structural damage; leave if the building is unsafe.
  • Use stairs, never lifts.
  • Listen to the radio; avoid rumours.
  • Do not enter damaged buildings to retrieve belongings.

If trapped

  • Cover your mouth and nose with cloth to avoid dust.
  • Avoid shouting for long periods; tap on pipes or walls in threes.
  • Conserve energy and air.
  • Use a torch sparingly; do not light matches.
  • Signal when you hear rescuers.

India-specific notes

  • Seismic zones II–V; Zone V includes parts of Kashmir, Himachal, Uttarakhand, northeast India, Kutch and Andaman.
  • Older masonry buildings are the biggest risk; retrofit or identify safe rooms.
  • NDRF and state disaster response teams handle major rescues; local volunteers should not enter collapsed structures.
  • Keep a pair of sturdy shoes and a torch by the bed.
