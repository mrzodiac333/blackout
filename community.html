Community & Mutual Aid

Build a neighbourhood network before a crisis: skills mapping, shared resources, communication, leadership and conflict resolution.

Planning Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

No household survives alone. A street or apartment block that knows each other, shares skills and communicates calmly is dramatically more resilient than a street of well-stocked strangers.

Starting a network

  • Start small: immediate neighbours, one conversation at a time.
  • Create a messaging group for routine notices and emergencies.
  • Hold one short meeting: who has medical training, tools, vehicles, generators, spare rooms.
  • Agree on a check-in time and a simple "all okay" signal.
  • Keep it practical and non-political; avoid doomsday talk that scares people away.

Leadership and decisions

  • Choose a coordinator, not a boss; rotate roles so no one is indispensable.
  • Make decisions transparently and record them.
  • Use simple majority for routine choices; protect minority needs.
  • Assign tasks by ability, not status.
  • Plan for disagreement: set a rule that safety decisions are made by the person with relevant expertise.

Conflict and fairness

  • Share resources by agreed rules, not by who shouts loudest.
  • Prioritise vulnerable people and medical needs.
  • Address rumours quickly with verified information.
  • De-escalate disputes; separate people and revisit later.
  • Never allow vigilante justice or scapegoating.
Barter and tradeIn prolonged disruption, skills and goods become currency. Keep small items for trade — batteries, soap, matches, salt, tools — but never trade critical supplies you need to survive.
