Security & Self-Defence

Home hardening, situational awareness, alarms, community coordination, de-escalation and legal self-defence — without weapons or vigilantism.

Skills & Security Last reviewed 2026-10-19 10 min read

Security in a crisis is mostly avoidance, awareness and community. Violence is rare but panic, rumours and opportunism are not. Plan to be boring, prepared and connected.

Legal and ethical

This guide does not provide weapon-making instructions. In India, firearms and many weapons are tightly regulated. Self-defence law permits reasonable force in imminent danger — it does not permit revenge, traps or vigilante violence. Follow the law.

Situational awareness

  • Know your exits wherever you are; identify two ways out.
  • Watch for behaviour, not appearance: loitering, following, unusual interest in your home.
  • Avoid crowds, protests and checkpoints; move around, not through.
  • Trust unease; leave early rather than explain later.
  • Keep your routines less predictable; vary routes and times.
  • Limit what you post online about supplies, travel and valuables — see opsec.

Hardening your home

  • Solid doors with deadbolts, security bars on ground-floor windows, and good lighting.
  • Trim bushes that hide entry points; keep the perimeter visible.
  • Use timers and motion lights to make the house look occupied.
  • Keep gates locked, tools and ladders secured, and fuel stored out of sight.
  • Install a door chain and peephole; verify identity before opening.
  • Prepare a safe room with water, a phone, a torch and a first-aid kit.
  • Know how to shut off gas and power in an emergency.

Neighbourhood coordination

  • Exchange phone numbers with immediate neighbours; create a simple WhatsApp or SMS group.
  • Agree on a check-in time and a signal for "I need help".
  • Map who has medical skills, tools, vehicles, generators or vulnerable members.
  • Organise a shared watch only if it is safe and lawful; never confront armed strangers.
  • Share information cautiously; rumours cause more harm than shortages.
  • Plan for pets and vulnerable neighbours.

De-escalation and self-defence

  • Avoid confrontation whenever possible; distance is the best defence.
  • Speak calmly, keep hands visible, and do not corner anyone.
  • Give an aggressor a way out; do not humiliate them.
  • If attacked, use the minimum force needed to escape and get help — then report it.
  • Legal self-defence tools in India are limited; carry a torch, whistle or personal alarm rather than an illegal weapon.
  • Never set traps, booby-traps or electrified fences — they are illegal and often hurt the innocent.
If you are followedCross the street, enter a shop or public place, call someone, and head toward people and light. Do not go home until you are sure you are not being followed.

Scams and misinformation

  • Be sceptical of urgent demands for money, donations or personal information.
  • Verify relief offers through official channels.
  • Do not share OTPs, bank details or copies of documents with strangers.
  • Check forwarding-chain messages before acting; official alerts come from official sources.
  • Report price gouging and fraud to local authorities.
