Food Production

Gardening, seed saving, composting, small livestock and fishing for longer-term food security.

Skills & Security Last reviewed 2026-10-19 8 min read

Stored food runs out. Growing food takes months, so start before you need it. Even a balcony can produce greens, herbs and tomatoes; a small plot can supplement a household.

Starting a garden

  • Sun: most vegetables need 6+ hours of direct light.
  • Soil: loose, dark and crumbly; add compost generously.
  • Water: deep watering less often beats shallow daily watering.
  • Easy crops: spinach, methi, coriander, beans, tomatoes, chillies, radish, pumpkin.
  • Containers: 30 cm depth for greens and herbs; deeper for tomatoes and root crops.
  • Mulch: reduces evaporation and weeds — critical in Indian heat.

Seed saving

  • Save from open-pollinated (non-hybrid) plants for reliable seed.
  • Choose the healthiest plants; let seeds mature fully on the plant.
  • Dry seeds thoroughly and store in a cool, dark, dry place with a desiccant.
  • Label with crop, variety and year.
  • Avoid saving seed from diseased plants; rotate crops to prevent soil-borne disease.

Composting

  • Mix greens (kitchen scraps, fresh leaves) with browns (dry leaves, cardboard, straw).
  • Keep it as damp as a wrung-out sponge; turn weekly for faster compost.
  • Do not compost meat, dairy, oily food or diseased plants.
  • Bury food scraps in a trench if you have no bin — it builds soil directly.
  • Compost needs 2–3 months in warm weather.

Small livestock and fishing

  • Chickens: eggs and pest control; need secure housing against predators and a daily routine.
  • Goats: milk and brush control; a serious daily commitment.
  • Bees: honey and pollination; requires training and calm handling.
  • Fishing: a line, hooks and local knowledge beat elaborate gear. Follow local laws and avoid polluted water.
  • Trapping: only legal, humane traps for food; never set traps where children or pets can reach them.
