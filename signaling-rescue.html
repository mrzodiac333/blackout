Signalling & Rescue

Attract attention when lost or trapped: visual signals, sound, radio, and ground-to-air markers.

Skills & Security Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read

Rescue depends on being found. Stay put when you can, make yourself visible, and conserve energy and water. Signal consistently — three of anything is the universal distress signal.

The rules of three

  • Three whistle blasts, three flashes, three fires — distress.
  • Six blasts per minute, then a minute's pause, is the international distress pattern.
  • Respond to another signal with the same pattern to acknowledge.
  • Conserve energy; do not shout continuously.

Visual signals

  • Mirror: flash sunlight toward aircraft or searchers; visible for kilometres.
  • Torch: flash SOS at night; conserve battery.
  • Bright cloth: tie to a high point or spread on open ground.
  • Smoke: white smoke for day (green leaves), black smoke for snow; build three fires.
  • Ground markers: large letters or symbols cleared in snow/sand; use international symbols (V = need help, X = need medical help).

Sound and radio

  • A whistle carries farther than a voice and costs little energy.
  • Bang pots or use a torch strobe to attract attention.
  • If you have a licensed radio, transmit on a recognised emergency frequency and follow net instructions.
  • If unlicensed, do not transmit on amateur or public-safety bands; use a phone, whistle or visual signals.
  • Leave a written note with your name, time, direction and condition.

Being rescued safely

  • Stay put once you have signalled; rescuers search where you were.
  • Make yourself visible and keep signalling until contact.
  • Do not approach helicopters from the rear; follow crew signals and secure loose items.
  • Conserve water and food; rest and stay warm.
  • If trapped, cover your mouth and nose, tap on structures in threes, and avoid shouting for long periods.
