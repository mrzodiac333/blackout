Family Emergency Plan

A one-page plan every household needs: meeting points, contacts, roles, evacuation routes and a 72-hour timeline.

Planning Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

The most valuable preparedness document is a single page that everyone knows. Fill it in, print copies, and practise it. Revisit it every six months.

Meeting points and contacts

  • Meeting point A: immediately outside your home, in the open, away from glass and trees.
  • Meeting point B: a nearby public place everyone can reach on foot, e.g. a school or temple.
  • Out-of-area contact: a relative or friend outside your city. Everyone memorises their number.
  • School and workplace plans: know their evacuation procedures and pickup points.
  • Write these on paper and keep copies in wallets, bags and the fridge.

Roles and responsibilities

Assign roles in advance. Everyone should know more than one role.

LeadDecides when to shelter or evacuate; carries the go-bag
CommsKeeps phones charged; contacts out-of-area person; monitors radio
MedicalCarries medical kit and records; knows medications
ChildrenStays with their adult; carries their own small bag
PetsCarries pet kit and leads animals
DocumentsCarries the document folder

Evacuation routes

  • Plan two routes out of your neighbourhood, in different directions.
  • Walk and drive them in advance; note bottlenecks and flood-prone underpasses.
  • Choose destinations: a relative's home, a shelter, or a safe town.
  • Agree on a trigger to leave early — e.g. an orange IMD warning or a mandatory order.
  • Keep the vehicle fuelled and the go-bag near the door.

The first 72 hours

Hour 0–1: safety

  • Get to safety; account for everyone; treat injuries.
  • Listen to official information; do not spread rumours.
  • Shut off gas/electricity if instructed or if you smell gas.

Hour 1–6: stabilise

  • Water and food; check on vulnerable neighbours.
  • Charge devices and radios; fill water containers.
  • Secure the home; check for damage and hazards.

Hour 6–24: communicate

  • Contact the out-of-area person; send status messages.
  • Decide whether to stay or leave.
  • Record decisions and information received.

Hour 24–72: sustain

  • Ration water and food; maintain hygiene.
  • Rest in shifts; keep a routine.
  • Reassess daily and adapt.

Draw your evacuation map

Keep a copy in the go-bag, one in the car and one on the fridge. Walk the routes with every household member, including children.
