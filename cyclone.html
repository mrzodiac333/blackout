Cyclone

Prepare for cyclones on India's coasts: evacuate early, avoid the eye's false calm, and survive the storm surge.

India Hazards Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

Cyclones bring wind, rain and storm surge — the rise in sea level that kills most people. India's east coast is especially exposed. Follow evacuation orders immediately.

Before landfall

  • Track IMD bulletins and colour warnings; prepare when orange is issued.
  • Evacuate if ordered, especially from low-lying coastal areas and kutcha houses.
  • Secure roofs with ropes or sandbags; board or tape windows.
  • Move valuables and documents to high ground; pack a go-bag.
  • Fill water containers; charge devices.
  • Move livestock to high ground; do not tie animals where they can drown.

During the storm

  • Stay indoors, away from windows; go to the strongest interior room.
  • If the eye passes, there is a false calm — do not go outside until the all-clear.
  • Keep the radio on; conserve phone battery.
  • Watch for flooding and structural damage; move to a higher floor if needed.
  • Do not touch downed power lines.
Storm surge

Surge can raise sea level by metres and arrive before the strongest winds. If you are near the coast and told to evacuate, go immediately.

After the cyclone

  • Wait for official clearance; roads and power lines may be down.
  • Check for injuries and structural damage; beware snakes and debris.
  • Boil or treat all drinking water.
  • Photograph damage for insurance; avoid scams.
  • Check on vulnerable neighbours.

India-specific notes

  • Cyclone season: pre-monsoon (April–June) and post-monsoon (October–December), mainly Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea.
  • States most affected: Odisha, West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Kerala.
  • NDRF and state teams pre-position before landfall; follow district collector instructions.
  • Cyclone shelters are public buildings; know the route.
