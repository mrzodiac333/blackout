Medications & Prescriptions

Stock, store and track essential medicines safely — without prescribing or self-medicating.

Medical Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

For people with chronic conditions, medicines are survival equipment. Plan a buffer, store them correctly, and keep records. This page is about management, not prescribing.

This is not medical training

Read this to recognise emergencies and act until help arrives — not to replace a certified course. Take a Red Cross or St John Ambulance first-aid and CPR class. Never perform procedures you have not been trained for.

Building a buffer

  • Ask your doctor for a longer prescription where appropriate and legal.
  • Refill early enough to build a gradual buffer — never skip doses to stockpile.
  • Keep at least 7 days in your go-bag and 30 days at home if possible.
  • Include over-the-counter essentials: ORS, pain/fever relief, antihistamine, antiseptic.
  • Keep a copy of prescriptions and a doctor's letter for controlled medicines.
Controlled medicines

Some medicines are legally restricted. Stockpiling or transporting them without documentation can be illegal. Follow your doctor's and pharmacist's guidance.

Storage and expiry

  • Store below 25 °C, away from light and moisture — not in a bathroom.
  • Keep medicines in original labelled containers.
  • Record expiry dates; use older stock first.
  • Most tablets remain effective past expiry but potency declines; do not rely on expired medicines for serious conditions. Never use expired insulin, liquids, or antibiotics.
  • Dispose of medicines safely — not down the drain; use a pharmacy take-back scheme where available.

Refrigerated medicines

Insulin and some biologics need 2–8 °C. During outages, use a cooler with ice packs and a thermometer; do not freeze. Have a plan with a neighbour, clinic or pharmacy.

Medication records

Keep a written card for each person, in the wallet and in the go-bag:

  • Name, blood group, allergies
  • Conditions and current medicines with doses and times
  • Prescribing doctor and pharmacy contacts
  • Vaccination history
  • Emergency contact

Photograph the cards and store the images offline on your phone.

Using medicines safely

  • Follow the prescribed dose exactly; do not double up after a missed dose unless your doctor says so.
  • Check for interactions with alcohol and other medicines.
  • Do not share prescription medicines.
  • Do not use antibiotics without a prescription — resistance and side effects are serious.
  • If someone has a reaction (rash, swelling, breathing difficulty), stop the medicine and seek urgent care.
