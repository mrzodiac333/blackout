Heatwave

Survive extreme heat: recognise heat illness, cool your home, and protect vulnerable people.

India Hazards Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read

Heatwaves are India's deadliest natural hazard by some counts. They are silent and slow. The old, the young, outdoor workers and the poor are most at risk.

Protecting yourself

  • Drink water before you are thirsty; add salt to food.
  • Stay indoors 11:00–16:00; avoid direct sun.
  • Wear loose, light-coloured clothing; use a hat and sunglasses.
  • Close curtains on sun-facing windows; open at night for ventilation.
  • Use wet cloths, cool showers and fans; a damp sheet over an open window cools incoming air.
  • Never leave anyone in a parked car.

Heat illness

Heat illness: recognise and act.
ConditionSignsAction
Heat crampsMuscle spasms, heavy sweatingRest in shade, fluids with salt/ORS
Heat exhaustionWeakness, dizziness, nausea, clammy skinCool, shade, ORS, monitor
Heat strokeHot skin, confusion, collapse, high temperatureCall 112; cool rapidly, do not give fluids if unconscious

Cooling without AC

  • Use cross-ventilation at night; close windows and curtains by day.
  • Create a cooler sleeping area on the lowest floor.
  • Hang damp sheets in front of windows; evaporation cools the air.
  • Use a hand fan or battery fan; charge during solar hours.
  • Take cool showers; keep a wet towel on the neck and wrists.
  • Cook early or outdoors to avoid heating the house.

India-specific notes

  • IMD issues heatwave alerts when temperatures exceed 40 °C in the plains or 30 °C in the hills.
  • States with severe heat: Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Odisha.
  • NDMA recommends avoiding outdoor work midday and checking on the elderly.
  • Water shortages often accompany heatwaves; store and treat water.
