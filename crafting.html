Crafting & Repair

Cordage, fire starters, shelter building, tool repair, sewing and repurposing — practical skills that multiply what you already own.

Skills & Security Last reviewed 2026-10-19 9 min read

Crafting in a crisis is about making what you need from what you have. Prioritise skills that use common materials and can be practised now: cordage, repair, fire, containers and shelter.

Cordage and knots

  • Paracord is strong, reusable and cheap; keep 30 m in your kit.
  • Natural cordage: nettle fibre, inner bark, and braided grass are workable but slow to make.
  • Three-knot baseline: bowline (fixed loop), taut-line hitch (adjustable tension), and sheet bend (join two ropes).
  • Inspect cord for abrasion and UV damage; retire damaged cord.

Fire starters and char cloth

  • Char cloth: cotton cloth baked in a sealed tin over a fire until black; catches a spark easily.
  • Petroleum-jelly cotton balls light reliably and burn for minutes.
  • Ferro rod works wet and lasts for thousands of strikes; practise before you need it.
  • Magnesium block shavings ignite with a spark.
  • Store tinder in a waterproof container and keep a lighter in every kit.

Repair skills

  • Sewing: buttons, seams, patches and gear repair; a heavy needle and waxed thread handle canvas.
  • Electrical: replacing plugs, splicing wires, soldering; always disconnect power first.
  • Plumbing: washers, tape and pipe clamps fix most leaks.
  • Woodwork: basic joints, wedges and handles.
  • Metalwork: filing, drilling and simple bending; rivets and bolts beat glue for load-bearing repairs.
  • Tape: duct tape and self-amalgamating tape fix an astonishing range of problems.

Repurposing safely

  • Containers: food-grade plastic only for food and water; never use chemical drums.
  • Metal cans: watch for sharp edges and lead solder; do not cook in cans with plastic linings.
  • Tires: excellent for sandals and fire rings; burning them releases toxic smoke.
  • Plastic bottles: cloches, funnels, drip irrigation; avoid using heated plastic for food.
  • Timber: never burn treated or painted wood indoors — toxic fumes.
