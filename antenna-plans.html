Antenna Build Plans

Printable plans for four practical antennas: half-wave dipole, quarter-wave ground plane, 2 m J-pole and a tape-measure Yagi.

Communication Last reviewed 2026-10-19 14 min read

These plans are for licensed amateur operators or receive-only use. Antennas are simple, cheap and effective — but they must be installed safely. Never install an antenna near power lines.

Before you build

You need a WPC amateur licence to transmit. Receive-only use of these antennas with a scanner or SDR is legal. All dimensions are starting points — trim and tune for lowest SWR with a meter.

Antenna length calculator

Enter a frequency in MHz to get starting lengths. Results are approximate; real installations need tuning.

Formulas: dipole total ≈ 143/f, quarter-wave ≈ 71.5/f, all in metres, f in MHz. Use the tools page for the full calculator.

Build steps

  1. Cut two equal wires about 3% longer than the table.
  2. Attach insulators at the outer ends and a centre insulator/balun.
  3. Solder or clamp the coax centre conductor to one leg and the shield to the other.
  4. Support the ends with rope; keep the wire taut but not tight enough to snap.
  5. Measure SWR at low power and trim both legs equally in small steps.

Tuning: if the resonant frequency is too low, shorten the legs; too high, lengthen them. Aim for SWR below 1.5:1.

Quarter-wave ground plane

Ground plane dimensions.

2 m (146 MHz)0.49 m0.51 m
70 cm (435 MHz)0.16 m0.17 m

Build steps

  1. Mount an SO-239 connector through a small metal plate or bracket.
  2. Solder the radiator to the centre pin; solder four radials to the shield/body.
  3. Bend radials down at about 45 degrees.
  4. Seal the connector with self-amalgamating tape.
  5. Mount on a mast clear of metal and wiring; check SWR and adjust radiator length.

Two-metre J-pole

J-pole dimensions for 146 MHz (copper pipe version).

Long radiator1480 mm

Gap at bottom50 mm
Feed point (from bottom of stub)≈ 60–90 mm, tune by sliding

Build steps

  1. Cut pipe or twin-lead to the dimensions; keep the two conductors parallel and spaced.
  2. Join the top of the stub to the radiator; leave the bottom gap open.
  3. Connect coax: centre to the radiator side, shield to the stub side, at the feed point.
  4. Seal the coax joint and mount the antenna vertically.
  5. Tune by moving the feed point for lowest SWR; mark the final position.

J-poles are DC-grounded at the feed and work well without a balun, but they still need weatherproofing and safe mounting.

Three-element tape-measure Yagi

Reflector1000 mm0 mm (rear)

Build steps

  1. Cut three elements from a steel tape measure; file the ends smooth.
  2. Mark the boom positions and drill clearance holes.
  3. Mount elements through insulating spacers so they do not short to the boom.
  4. Feed the driven element at its centre with coax; a simple gamma match or balun improves matching.
  5. Aim the director toward the target and tune SWR; keep the antenna away from metal and your body.
Directional tipsYagis are directional: point the director (shortest element) toward the signal. Elevate slightly for distant repeaters. Handheld use detunes the antenna — mount it on a short mast or boom.

Safety, grounding and weatherproofing

  • Power lines: keep every part of the antenna and mast at least twice its height away from power lines. Assume lines are live.
  • Lightning: disconnect coax and ground the mast when storms are forecast. Do not rely on a surge protector alone.
  • Grounding: bond the mast and coax shield to a separate earth rod; do not use the mains earth as your RF earth.
  • Weatherproofing: wrap connectors with self-amalgamating tape, then electrical tape; seal coax entry points.
  • Mechanical: use guy ropes or a proper mast; wind loading on a wet antenna is significant.
  • RF exposure: do not transmit with people close to the antenna, especially at high power.
  • SWR: below 1.5:1 good, 1.5–2.0:1 usable, above 2.0:1 stop and fix.
