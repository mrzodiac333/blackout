Act now

Immediate actions for the first minutes. Find the situation, open it and act. Call 112 early if life is at risk.

Immediate actionsLast reviewed 2026-10-19
Life-threatening emergency

Call 112

Police, fire and ambulance. Follow the operator's instructions. If you cannot speak safely, use the emergency SMS or app service available in your area.

Medical emergencies

Call 112 early. Do not delay help while looking for a perfect answer.

Severe bleeding
  • Call 112. Press hard on the wound with a clean cloth or dressing. Keep pressing.
  • Add more layers if blood soaks through; do not remove the first layer.
  • If bleeding from an arm or leg is life-threatening and you are trained, use a tourniquet above the wound and note the time.
  • Keep the person warm and lying down. Watch for shock.
Full bleeding first aid →
Not breathing or unresponsive
  • Call 112. If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, start CPR.
  • Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest at 100–120 compressions per minute.
  • Use an AED if one is available and follow its voice prompts.
  • Swap with another trained person every two minutes if possible.
Full CPR guidance →
Choking
  • If they can cough, speak or breathe, encourage coughing.
  • If they cannot breathe, speak or cough, give 5 back blows between the shoulder blades.
  • If not cleared, give 5 abdominal thrusts. Repeat the cycle.
  • Call 112 if the object does not come out or the person collapses.
Full choking guidance →
Burns
  • Stop the burning. Remove hot or smouldering clothing unless it is stuck to the skin.
  • Cool the burn with cool running water for 20 minutes. Do not use ice, butter or toothpaste.
  • Cover loosely with clean, non-stick cloth.
  • Seek urgent care for large, deep, facial, hand, joint or electrical burns.
Full burn first aid →
Heat stroke
  • Call 112. Move the person to shade or a cool room.
  • Remove excess clothing and cool aggressively with water and fanning.
  • Do not give fluids to a confused or unconscious person.
  • Heat stroke is a medical emergency; do not wait for the person to “cool down naturally”.
Heat illness guidance →
Snakebite
  • Call 112 and get to a hospital with antivenom.
  • Keep the person calm and still. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing.
  • Immobilise the limb and keep it below heart level if possible.
  • Do not cut, suck, apply a tight bandage or try to catch the snake.
Full snakebite guidance →

Natural hazards

Move first, then refine. Do not wait for perfect information.

Flood
  • Move to higher ground early. Do not wait for the water to reach the stairs.
  • Never walk or drive through moving water. Turn around.
  • Avoid bridges, underpasses, riverbanks and electrical equipment.
  • Drink only treated water. Flood water contaminates everything it touches.
Full flood playbook →
Earthquake
  • Drop, cover and hold on. Stay away from windows and tall furniture.
  • If outdoors, move away from buildings, walls and power lines.
  • After shaking, check for injuries, gas leaks and structural damage. Expect aftershocks.
  • Do not use lifts. Evacuate only if the building is unsafe.
Full earthquake playbook →
Cyclone
  • Follow evacuation orders. Move to a sturdy shelter away from the coast.
  • Stay away from windows. Shelter in an interior room on the lowest safe floor.
  • Avoid the calm eye; the wind will return from the other direction.
  • Keep away from downed power lines and flood water.
Full cyclone playbook →
Wildfire
  • Leave early if warned. Do not wait for the fire to arrive.
  • Close windows and vents. Protect your lungs with a mask or damp cloth.
  • Choose a route away from smoke and active fire.
  • Follow fire-service instructions; do not return until it is declared safe.
Full wildfire playbook →
Landslide
  • Move sideways, away from the slope. Do not try to outrun a slide downhill.
  • If you cannot escape, curl up and protect your head.
  • Stay alert for more slides; they often repeat.
  • Stay away from the toe of the slope and damaged buildings.
Full landslide playbook →
Tsunami
  • Move inland or to high ground immediately. Do not wait for an official warning.
  • Natural warnings include strong shaking, a sudden sea retreat or a loud roar.
  • Stay away from the coast until authorities say it is safe; waves can continue for hours.
  • Do not return to the beach to watch.
Full tsunami playbook →

Utilities and shelter

Assume the system is unsafe until you can verify it.

Downed power line or outage
  • Treat every downed line as live. Stay at least 10 metres away and keep others back.
  • Do not touch fences, water or vehicles near a line.
  • Report it to the utility and emergency services.
  • Use torches, not candles. Never run a generator indoors.
Power safety guide →
Contaminated water
  • Assume flood water is contaminated. Do not drink it.
  • Boil water for at least 1 minute at a rolling boil, or use a proven treatment method.
  • Do not use water that smells of fuel or chemicals.
  • Store treated water in clean, covered containers.
Water treatment guide →
Gas leak
  • Do not switch lights, fans or appliances on or off. Do not use a phone inside.
  • Open windows and doors if safe, then leave the building.
  • Turn off the gas supply at the cylinder or meter if you can do so safely.
  • Call the gas emergency service from outside. Keep flames and sparks away.
Fire and gas safety →
Trapped or buried
  • Stay calm and conserve air and energy. Cover your mouth and nose against dust.
  • Tap on a pipe or wall in a regular pattern. Use a whistle or torch if available.
  • Do not light a flame; there may be gas.
  • Signal rescuers and avoid unnecessary movement.
Signalling and rescue →

Security

Avoid confrontation. Escape, shelter and call for help when it is safe.

Violence or intruder
  • Get to a safe room. Lock or barricade the door and stay quiet.
  • Call 112 when it is safe; leave the line open if instructed.
  • Do not confront an armed intruder. Escape if you can do so without crossing their path.
  • De-escalate only if escape and shelter are not possible.
Security and de-escalation →
Civil unrest
  • Avoid crowds, rumours and flashpoints. Keep indoors if it is safe.
  • Monitor official alerts and tell someone your plan.
  • Choose a safe route and avoid roads that may be blocked.
  • Do not carry anything that could escalate a confrontation.
