Reading Weather Without Forecasts

Use clouds, wind, pressure and animal behaviour to anticipate weather — and know IMD's colour codes.

Skills & Security Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read

Forecasts are better than folklore, but when the network is down, the sky still tells you a lot. Learn the basics and keep a barometer if you can.

Clouds and wind

  • Cumulonimbus (tall, anvil-topped): thunderstorms, hail, heavy rain — shelter early.
  • Cirrus thickening and lowering: a front may be approaching within 12–24 hours.
  • Dark, low clouds with gusty wind: squall or storm imminent.
  • Wind shifting and strengthening often precedes a storm; note direction changes.
  • Calm, humid and oppressive conditions can precede severe thunderstorms.

Pressure and humidity

  • Falling pressure generally means worsening weather; a rapid fall warns of a storm.
  • Rising pressure usually means improving weather.
  • High humidity, fog and dew point close to temperature increase fog and rain likelihood.
  • A barometer trend is more useful than a single reading.

IMD alerts

IMD colour-coded warnings.
ColourMeaningAction
GreenNo severe weather expectedNormal activity
YellowBe awareMonitor updates; secure loose items
OrangeBe preparedPrepare to shelter; avoid travel if possible
RedTake actionShelter, follow official instructions, avoid travel

IMD issues warnings for cyclones, heavy rain, thunderstorms, heatwaves, cold waves and fog. Check mausam.imd.gov.in when connectivity exists.

Local signs and limits

  • Ants moving nests, birds flying low, and livestock seeking shelter are weak but sometimes useful signs.
  • Folk sayings vary by region and are not reliable for severe weather.
  • Do not rely on a single sign — combine observations with official warnings.
  • In coastal areas, know cyclone season and evacuation routes; a storm surge is the biggest killer.
