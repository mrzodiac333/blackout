Food: Stock, Preserve, Cook

Build a two-week food stock you will actually eat, preserve food safely, and cook without mains power.

Survival Last reviewed 2026-10-19 9 min read

Food security is mostly logistics, not survival foraging. Store what you eat, eat what you store, and rotate it. Aim for two weeks of calories per person, plus a way to cook without grid power.

Buy one extra of each staple per shopping trip until you reach your target. This spreads the cost and keeps stock fresh.

Rotation and storage

  • First in, first out. New items go behind old ones; write dates on everything.
  • Store in airtight containers — steel, food-grade plastic, or mylar bags with oxygen absorbers for long-term grain.
  • Keep cool, dark and dry; heat and humidity destroy food and invite pests.
  • Inspect monthly for insects, damp or swollen cans. Discard bulging cans.
  • Keep a written inventory on the inside of the pantry door.
Never eat

Food from bulging, leaking or badly dented cans, food with mould beyond the surface of hard cheese or bread, or anything with an off smell you cannot explain. When in doubt, throw it out.

Preserving food

Drying

Slice thinly and dry in sun, a solar dryer, or a low oven. Drying works best in low humidity. Store in airtight containers; add a moisture absorber if available. Vegetables and fruit rehydrate well; meat drying requires care and is not recommended without training.

Salting and curing

Salt draws out moisture and inhibits bacteria. Use non-iodised salt for curing where possible, keep the food fully covered, and store cool. Uneven curing leaves pockets where bacteria survive.

Pickling

Vinegar or a strong salt brine plus acid (lemon) preserves vegetables. Keep vegetables submerged. Use clean jars and lids; discard if cloudy, mouldy or fizzy.

Canning

Low-acid foods (meat, beans, vegetables) require a pressure canner to reach 116–121 °C. Boiling-water canning is only safe for high-acid foods (fruit, pickles, tomatoes with added acid). Improper canning causes botulism, which can be fatal. If you have not been trained, do not attempt low-acid canning.

Botulism

Never taste food from a jar with a bulging lid, a broken seal, or an off smell. Boiling does not reliably destroy botulinum toxin already formed in food. Discard it.

Cooking without power

  • Camp stove + fuel canisters. Most reliable; use outdoors or in a ventilated space only.
  • LPG cylinder. Store upright, secure, and check the hose; never store indoors in a bedroom.
  • Rocket stove or wood fire. Efficient with small sticks; use outdoors.
  • Solar cooker. Slow but fuel-free on clear days.
  • Thermal cooking (haybox). Bring food to a boil, then insulate the pot for hours — saves fuel for rice, dal and stews.
Carbon monoxide

Never burn charcoal, wood or LPG in an enclosed room, tent or garage. Carbon monoxide is invisible and odourless and kills quickly. Always ventilate.

Cooking also purifies water and makes food safer; a hot meal is a major morale boost. Keep a manual can opener, matches in a waterproof bag, and a lighter in every kit.

Foraging — with extreme caution

Foraging is a supplement, not a plan. Many edible-looking plants are toxic, and misidentification can be fatal.

  • Learn a small number of unmistakable local plants from a qualified person, not from photos alone.
  • Avoid plants with milky sap, umbrella-shaped flower heads (hemlock family), or unknown berries.
  • Do not eat mushrooms unless identified by an expert in person.
  • Wash all forage and treat water used to wash it.
  • Avoid foraging near roads, industrial sites and sprayed fields.
