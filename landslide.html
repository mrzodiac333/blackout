Landslide

Recognise landslide warning signs in hill country and know how to react when the ground moves.

India Hazards Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read

Landslides kill in the Himalayan states and the Western Ghats every monsoon, often after heavy rain. Warning signs appear hours or minutes before a slope fails — learn them.

Warning signs

  • New cracks or bulges in the ground, roads or walls.
  • Doors and windows sticking suddenly.
  • Tilting trees, poles or fences.
  • Springs or seeps appearing where none were before.
  • Rumbling or cracking sounds; faint vibrations.
  • Muddy water or debris suddenly in a stream.
  • Fences, retaining walls or stairs moving away from structures.

During a landslide

  • Move away from the path of the slide — sideways and uphill, not downhill.
  • If you cannot escape, curl into a tight ball and protect your head.
  • Stay away from the slide's edges and the valley below; more debris may follow.
  • Do not cross a slide; the ground may be unstable.
  • Listen for trapped people and call for help; do not dig without training.

Preparing your home

  • Avoid building below steep slopes or in old slide paths.
  • Maintain drainage; water is the main trigger.
  • Do not cut trees on steep slopes; roots hold soil.
  • Build retaining walls and check them before monsoon.
  • Plan an evacuation route to high, stable ground.
  • Evacuate early if heavy rain persists and you see warning signs.

India-specific notes

  • High-risk areas: Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Sikkim, Arunachal, Nagaland, Kerala and the Western Ghats.
  • Monsoon rainfall is the main trigger; earthquakes also destabilise slopes.
  • NDRF and state teams carry out rescues; local volunteers should not enter slide zones.
  • Roads in hill areas can be blocked for days; keep extra supplies.
