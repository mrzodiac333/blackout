Transport & Evacuation

Vehicle preparation, fuel storage, evacuation routes and how to move safely on foot or by bicycle.

Survival Last reviewed 2026-10-19 8 min read

Evacuation is a decision, not a default. When you must move, the goal is to leave early, light and with a destination — not to join a panicked exodus on a blocked highway.

Vehicle readiness

  • Keep at least half a tank; refuel early, not when everyone else does.
  • Service the vehicle, check tyres, coolant, oil and brakes.
  • Carry a jump starter, tyre inflator, tow strap, spare tyre and tools.
  • Keep water, blankets, a torch, first-aid kit, phone charger and a paper map in the car.
  • Store fuel only in approved containers, secured and ventilated — never in the passenger cabin.
Flood water

Never drive through moving water. 30 cm of moving water can move a car; 60 cm can float it. Turn around, don't drown.

Routes and timing

  • Leave before official evacuation orders become mandatory if you have vulnerable people, pets or medical needs.
  • Use secondary roads; highways become car parks.
  • Avoid bridges, underpasses and river crossings in flood conditions.
  • Drive with headlights on, keep space, and never stop in the road to film.
  • Tell your out-of-area contact your route and expected arrival time.

On foot and by bicycle

  • A bicycle is the most efficient evacuation vehicle for most city distances — quiet, fast, easy to repair.
  • On foot, carry weight on your hips, not shoulders; 10–15% of body weight is a realistic load.
  • Walk early and rest during heat; drink before thirst.
  • Wear broken-in shoes, a hat and high-visibility clothing if moving near roads.
  • Avoid crowds, checkpoints and confrontation; move around, not through.

Pets and livestock

  • Prepare a pet go-bag: food, water, bowl, leash, carrier, vaccination record, medications.
  • Many shelters do not accept animals; identify pet-friendly shelters or boarding in advance.
  • For livestock, plan water, feed and transport; evacuate early.
  • Never leave animals locked in a vehicle in heat.
