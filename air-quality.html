Air Quality & Smog

Protect your lungs during severe smog, dust storms and wildfire smoke — indoors and outdoors.

India Hazards Last reviewed 2026-10-19 5 min read

Air pollution is a slow disaster that becomes acute in winter across north India. Fine particles (PM2.5) penetrate deep into the lungs and blood. Protect the vulnerable first.

Outdoors

  • Check the AQI; avoid outdoor exercise when it is high.
  • Wear a well-fitted N95/FFP2 mask; cloth and surgical masks do not filter PM2.5 well.
  • Avoid busy roads; walk on quieter streets away from traffic.
  • Keep car windows closed and use recirculated air.
  • Do not burn waste or use fireworks during smog episodes.

Indoors

  • Keep windows and doors closed on high-pollution days.
  • Use an air purifier with a HEPA filter in the room you use most.
  • Create a clean room: seal gaps, run a purifier, keep it closed.
  • Avoid incense, candles and frying that add indoor particles.
  • Wet-mop floors rather than sweeping dry dust.

Health effects

  • Irritated eyes, nose and throat; coughing and wheezing.
  • Worsening asthma and COPD; chest pain.
  • Increased risk of heart attack and stroke.
  • Children and the elderly are most vulnerable.
  • Seek care for severe breathlessness, chest pain or blue lips.

India-specific notes

  • North India smog peaks October–January due to stubble burning, traffic, industry and weather.
  • Delhi, Gurugram, Noida, Ghaziabad and Faridabad regularly record severe AQI.
  • GRAP restrictions on construction, traffic and schools are imposed by the CAQM.
  • Dust storms in Rajasthan and Gujarat reduce visibility and worsen air quality.
