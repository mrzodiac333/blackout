First Aid Basics

Life-saving basics: severe bleeding, CPR, choking, burns, fractures, shock and the recovery position.

Medical Last reviewed 2026-10-19 11 min read

In the first minutes, simple actions save lives. Learn these skills hands-on, then use this page as a refresher.

This is not medical training

Read this to recognise emergencies and act until help arrives — not to replace a certified course. Take a Red Cross or St John Ambulance first-aid and CPR class. Never perform procedures you have not been trained for.

Quick reference card

Approach and primary survey

  1. Safety: traffic, fire, electricity, unstable structures, animals. Do not become a second casualty.
  2. Response: shout and tap the shoulders.
  3. Airway: open the airway; look for obvious obstruction.
  4. Breathing: look, listen, feel for up to 10 seconds.
  5. Circulation: check for severe bleeding.
  6. Call 112 and send for an AED if available.
ConsentIdentify yourself and ask for consent if the person is conscious. If unconscious, proceed under implied consent.

Severe bleeding

  1. Put on gloves if available.
  2. Apply firm direct pressure with a sterile dressing or clean cloth.
  3. Do not lift the dressing to check; add more layers if it soaks through.
  4. If bleeding continues from a limb and you are trained, apply a tourniquet 5–7 cm above the wound, tighten until bleeding stops, and note the time.
  5. Keep the person warm and lying down; treat for shock.
  6. Get to hospital immediately.
Do not

Do not remove an embedded object — pad around it. Do not use a tourniquet on the neck or head. Do not loosen a tourniquet once applied unless medical staff tell you to.

CPR and AED

For an adult who is unresponsive and not breathing normally:

  1. Call 112 and get an AED.
  2. Place the heel of your hand in the centre of the chest; interlock fingers.
  3. Push hard and fast — 5–6 cm deep, 100–120 compressions per minute.
  4. Allow full recoil between compressions.
  5. If trained, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions; if not, continue compressions only.
  6. Use the AED as soon as it arrives and follow its voice prompts.
  7. Continue until help arrives, the person starts breathing, or you are exhausted.

For children and infants, use one hand or two fingers and gentler depth; follow the AED prompts. Hands-only CPR is appropriate if you are untrained or unwilling to give breaths.

Choking

Adult or child conscious: encourage coughing. If the airway is blocked, give up to 5 back blows between the shoulder blades, then up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich). Alternate until the object clears or the person becomes unresponsive, then start CPR.

Infant: 5 back blows face-down on your forearm, then 5 chest thrusts with two fingers. Do not use abdominal thrusts on infants.

Unconscious: call 112, start CPR, and check the mouth for visible objects only.

Burns

  1. Stop the burning: remove the source, smother flames, or rinse chemicals with copious water.
  2. Cool the burn with cool running water for 20 minutes; never use ice.
  3. Remove jewellery and clothing that is not stuck to the skin.
  4. Cover with a sterile non-stick dressing or cling film.
  5. Do not pop blisters or apply toothpaste, butter, oil or turmeric.
  6. Seek care for burns larger than the person's palm, burns on the face, hands, feet, joints or genitals, electrical burns, chemical burns, or any burn in a child or elderly person.
Electrical burns

Switch off the power before touching the person. Electrical burns can cause internal injury and heart rhythm problems — always seek medical care.

Fractures, sprains and head injury

Fractures

  • Do not move a suspected spinal injury unless there is immediate danger.
  • Support the limb in the position found; immobilise with a splint extending beyond the joints above and below.
  • Check circulation beyond the injury (colour, warmth, pulse) before and after splinting.
  • Apply cold packs wrapped in cloth for 15–20 minutes for sprains; elevate and rest.

Head injury

Seek emergency care for any loss of consciousness, vomiting, worsening headache, confusion, unequal pupils, clear fluid from nose/ears, or seizures. Keep the person still and monitor breathing.

Shock and the recovery position

Shock signs: pale, cold, clammy skin; rapid weak pulse; rapid breathing; thirst; confusion; anxiety.

  • Lay the person down and raise the legs unless a spinal or leg injury is suspected.
  • Keep warm with blankets; do not overheat.
  • Do not give food or drink.
  • Control bleeding and treat the cause; call 112.

Recovery position for an unconscious but breathing person: lie on the side, lower arm forward, upper leg bent, head tilted slightly back, mouth downward to allow drainage. Monitor breathing continuously.
