Scenario: Nuclear & EMP Basics

What to do in a radiation release or an electromagnetic pulse event — shelter, distance, time and information.

Scenarios Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

Nuclear and EMP events are low-probability but high-consequence. The core principles are simple: get inside, stay inside, stay tuned — and for EMP, protect the electronics that matter most.

Follow official instructions

In any radiological emergency, official guidance overrides this page. Do not evacuate into the path of a plume unless authorities tell you to.

Radiological release

  1. Get inside a building, ideally a basement or interior room with few windows.
  2. Stay inside for at least 24 hours, or until authorities say it is safe.
  3. Seal windows, doors and vents; turn off fans and AC.
  4. Remove outer clothing and bag it; shower with soap and water if exposed.
  5. Stay tuned to official radio and alerts.

Distance, shielding and time reduce exposure. Dense materials (concrete, earth, brick) shield best. Do not go outside to look for the source.

Electromagnetic pulse

  • An EMP can damage electronics over a wide area, especially connected to long wires and antennas.
  • Unplug devices and disconnect antennas if warned.
  • Store critical electronics in a Faraday bag or metal box; a microwave can work as a makeshift enclosure but is not guaranteed.
  • Keep paper maps, printed contacts and cash — they are immune.
  • A vehicle with electronic ignition may fail; older mechanical vehicles are more resilient.
  • Plan for communications loss and grid failure — see grid collapse.

Preparation

  • Two weeks of water, food and medicine.
  • Battery radio and spare batteries.
  • Plastic sheeting and tape for sealing a room.
  • Printed documents, maps and this guide.
  • A Faraday bag for a phone, radio and flashlight.
  • Cash in small notes.

Myths and limits

  • Iodine tablets protect only the thyroid and only against radioactive iodine; they are not a general anti-radiation pill. Take them only on official advice.
  • You cannot outrun fallout; sheltering is usually safer than fleeing.
  • An EMP is not the same as a nuclear blast; effects differ by altitude and device.
  • Preparedness for grid failure, communications loss and sheltering covers most of the practical response.
