Frequently Asked Questions

Honest answers about what this guide can and cannot do, how to start preparing, and the limits of DIY survival advice.

Start Here Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read

These are the questions people ask first. If you only read one page before starting, read this and the family plan.

Where do I start?

In this order: water, then communications, then food, then power. A household with 72 hours of water, a charged radio and a family meeting point is better prepared than one with a year of freeze-dried food and no plan. Work through the guides directory from the Start Here category.

How much should I store?

The Indian government and international agencies recommend at least 72 hours of self-sufficiency for households, and two weeks is a sensible personal target. Cyclones, floods and landslides can isolate communities for longer. Use the calculators to size water and food storage for your household.

Do I need a licence to use a walkie-talkie in India?

It depends on the device. Only WPC-approved, low-power 446 MHz units with Equipment Type Approval are licence-free. Anything that transmits on amateur, business or public-safety bands requires a licence. Imported FRS/GMRS radios sold online are usually not legal to transmit with in India. See Communication & radio.

Can this site teach me to treat wounds or perform procedures?

No. It teaches recognition, first aid and when to evacuate. It does not teach surgery, prescribing, dosing, or improvised procedures. Take a certified first-aid and CPR course — the medical hub lists what to learn and how to find training.

Do you cover weapons?

No weapon-making, explosives or illegal-device instructions. Security content focuses on awareness, hardening your home, alarms, community coordination, de-escalation and legal self-defence options. See Security.

Is an apocalypse realistic?

Total societal collapse is unlikely. Localised disasters are not: floods, cyclones, earthquakes, fires, grid failures and disease outbreaks happen every year in India. Preparing for a bad week also prepares you for a very bad month. Treat worst-case planning as insurance, not prophecy.

What if I have no money to prepare?

Start with free actions: learn your evacuation routes, agree a meeting point, learn to purify water by boiling, save documents in a waterproof bag, charge a power bank, and learn basic first aid. Store a little extra of what you already eat. Preparedness is a habit more than a shopping list.
