Wound Care & Infection

Clean, dress and monitor wounds when medical care is delayed — and know the signs that demand evacuation.

Medical Last reviewed 2026-10-19 8 min read

Most wounds heal with thorough cleaning and protection. Infection is the main danger when care is delayed. Clean early, cover properly, and check daily.

This is not medical training

Read this to recognise emergencies and act until help arrives — not to replace a certified course. Take a Red Cross or St John Ambulance first-aid and CPR class. Never perform procedures you have not been trained for.

Cleaning a wound

  1. Wash your hands and put on gloves.
  2. Stop bleeding with direct pressure.
  3. Rinse the wound with clean running water or saline for several minutes; use soap around the wound.
  4. Remove visible dirt with clean tweezers; do not dig deep into tissue.
  5. Apply an antiseptic (povidone-iodine or chlorhexidine) around the wound edges.
  6. Cover with a sterile non-stick dressing; change daily or when wet/soiled.
Do not

Do not pour hydrogen peroxide or alcohol into deep wounds — it damages tissue. Do not close deep, dirty or bite wounds yourself. Do not use cotton wool directly on the wound; fibres stick.

Special wounds

Animal biteWash 15 minutes with soap and water, antiseptic, same-day rabies care
BurnCool 20 minutes, non-stick dressing, no ice or toothpaste
Crush injuryControl bleeding, immobilise, watch for shock, evacuate
Embedded objectDo not remove; pad around and stabilise

Infection warning signs

  • Increasing pain, swelling, redness or warmth
  • Pus or foul smell
  • Red streaks spreading from the wound
  • Fever or chills
  • Swollen lymph nodes
  • Worsening general condition or confusion

Any of these means seek medical care immediately. Do not try to treat spreading infection with leftover antibiotics.

Tetanus and vaccination

Tetanus is caused by bacteria in soil and manure entering wounds. It is preventable by vaccination and can be fatal. Check your tetanus status before an emergency; booster doses are recommended every 10 years. Deep or dirty wounds may need a booster within 48 hours — see a doctor.

When to evacuate

  • Bleeding you cannot control
  • Deep or gaping wounds needing closure
  • Wounds with embedded objects or significant contamination
  • Bites, especially from unvaccinated animals
  • Any sign of infection or fever
  • Burns beyond minor size, or on sensitive areas
  • Loss of movement, sensation or circulation beyond the wound
