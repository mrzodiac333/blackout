Heat, Cold & Severe Weather

Recognise and treat heat illness and hypothermia, and prepare for India's heatwaves, cold snaps and storms.

Survival Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

India's climate ranges from Himalayan cold to coastal heat and monsoon humidity. Weather is the most common disaster driver in the country. Know the signs of heat illness and cold injury; both escalate fast.

Heat illness

Heat illness progression. Heat stroke is a medical emergency — call 112.
ConditionSignsAction
Heat crampsPainful muscle spasms, heavy sweatingRest, shade, fluids with salt/ORS
Heat exhaustionWeakness, dizziness, nausea, headache, cool clammy skinShade, cool, loosen clothing, sip ORS, monitor
Heat strokeHot skin, confusion, collapse, high temperatureCall 112; cool rapidly with water/ice, fan, do not give fluids if unconscious

Prevention: drink before thirst, avoid midday exertion, wear light loose clothing, check on the elderly and children, and never leave anyone in a parked car.

Cold injury

Hypothermia

Signs: shivering, confusion, slurred speech, clumsiness, drowsiness. Move to shelter, remove wet clothes, insulate from the ground, wrap in dry layers, share body heat. Warm the core first. Give warm sweet drinks only if fully alert.

Frostbite

Signs: pale, numb, hard skin. Do not rub. Warm gradually in 37–39 °C water if there is no risk of refreezing; protect the area and seek medical help.

Cold snaps in northern India kill people every winter, especially outdoors and in unheated homes. Keep dry layers, blankets and a safe heat source.

Storms, lightning and wind

  • Secure loose outdoor items before a storm; move indoors.
  • Stay away from windows and doors; avoid concrete floors and walls during lightning.
  • Do not shelter under isolated trees or hold metal objects outdoors.
  • Unplug sensitive electronics before a storm if time permits.
  • After a storm, watch for downed power lines — assume they are live.
  • Use IMD colour-coded warnings: yellow = be aware, orange = be prepared, red = take action.

Air quality and dust

  • During severe smog, stay indoors, close windows, and run a purifier if you have one.
  • Wear an N95/FFP2 mask outdoors, fitted to the face.
  • Avoid outdoor exercise; keep children and the elderly indoors.
  • During dust storms, seal gaps and cover food and water.
