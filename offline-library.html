Build an Offline Library

Keep the knowledge you need when the internet is down: printed binders, offline apps, USB archives and power-efficient devices.

Planning Last reviewed 2026-10-19 6 min read

The internet is a single point of failure. A small, well-chosen offline library — printed and digital — keeps critical knowledge available when networks fail.

What to keep

  • This guide, printed or saved for offline reading.
  • Offline maps of your city and region.
  • First-aid and medical reference cards.
  • Local emergency numbers and radio frequencies.
  • Documents and family plan.
  • Practical skills: knots, repairs, food preservation, gardening.
  • A few books for morale.

Printed library

  • Use a ring binder with plastic sleeves; it survives damp better than loose paper.
  • Print the most critical pages: first aid, water treatment, contacts, family plan, antenna plans.
  • Keep one copy at home and one in the go-bag.
  • Store in a sealed plastic bag with a desiccant.
  • Update pages as the site updates.

Digital library

  • Save pages as PDF or use the site's offline PWA install.
  • Download offline maps for your whole region, not just your city.
  • Keep an encrypted USB drive with documents, maps and references.
  • Store a copy with a trusted relative outside your area.
  • Test your offline library before you need it — put the phone in airplane mode and find the guide.

Powering your library

  • An e-ink reader lasts weeks on a charge and is ideal for reading.
  • A tablet with a large battery and a solar panel covers maps and PDFs.
  • Keep cables with each device and a power bank dedicated to the library.
  • Download content while you have connectivity; do not rely on streaming.
