Vulnerable Groups

Planning for children, older adults, people with disabilities, pregnant women and those with chronic illness.

Medical Last reviewed 2026-10-19 8 min read

Standard plans fail the people who need them most. Build your household plan around the person with the greatest needs, not the average adult.

This is not medical training

Read this to recognise emergencies and act until help arrives — not to replace a certified course. Take a Red Cross or St John Ambulance first-aid and CPR class. Never perform procedures you have not been trained for.

Children

  • Store formula, bottles, nappies and wipes for at least two weeks.
  • Keep oral rehydration salts and a thermometer.
  • Maintain routines and reassure; children read adult anxiety closely.
  • Practise meeting points and who to call.
  • Keep a current photo and identification in their bag.

Older adults

  • Extra medications, glasses, hearing-aid batteries and mobility aids.
  • Consider heat and cold sensitivity; check on them twice daily in extreme weather.
  • Keep a list of conditions, allergies and doctors.
  • Plan for moving someone with limited mobility — identify helpers in advance.
  • Social isolation is a health risk; include them in planning and daily contact.

People with disabilities

  • Ask the person what they need; do not assume.
  • Store spare batteries, chargers, catheters, hearing-aid supplies or other consumables.
  • Plan accessible evacuation routes and transport.
  • Keep a communication card if speech is affected.
  • Ensure shelters and toilets are accessible; advocate if they are not.

Pregnancy and infants

  • Know the nearest hospital with maternity services and a backup.
  • Keep antenatal records, medications and a delivery kit.
  • Plan transport that works at any hour; identify a neighbour with a vehicle.
  • For infants: formula, sterile bottles, nappies, blankets, ORS.
  • Signs needing urgent care: bleeding, severe headache, blurred vision, reduced fetal movement, contractions before term, fever.
  • See emergency childbirth for immediate support only — it is not a substitute for maternity care.

Chronic illness

  • Keep a 30-day medicine buffer where possible and a written care plan.
  • Store devices and supplies: glucose meter, inhaler, oxygen, dialysis information.
  • Know which hospitals can provide your treatment.
  • Wear a medical ID or carry a card.
  • Include your condition in the family plan and tell your support network.
