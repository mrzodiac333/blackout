Fire: Start, Control, Cook

Reliable fire lighting in wet conditions, safe fire management, and using fire for warmth, cooking and signalling.

Survival Last reviewed 2026-10-19 7 min read

Fire provides warmth, cooking, water purification, light and a signal. It also causes house fires, burns and carbon-monoxide deaths. The skill is not just lighting one — it is controlling it.

Preparation: tinder, kindling, fuel

  • Tinder: dry grass, birch bark, char cloth, cotton with petroleum jelly, dryer lint.
  • Kindling: pencil-thin dry twigs, split small wood.
  • Fuel: dry dead wood, sorted by size.
  • Ignition: lighter, ferro rod, matches in a waterproof case, magnifying lens.

In rain, look for dead branches still attached to trees, or split wet logs to reach dry wood inside.

Lighting a fire

  1. Clear a circle at least 3 metres wide down to bare soil; never light under overhanging branches.
  2. Build a small tinder bundle; light it and add kindling gradually.
  3. Add larger wood only once the kindling is burning steadily.
  4. Keep a water container and a shovel or sand nearby.
Indoors

Never use charcoal or wood fires indoors, in tents, or in enclosed courtyards without ventilation. Carbon monoxide kills silently. Use a battery CO alarm where possible.

Fire safety at home

  • Keep extinguishers on every floor; know PASS — Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep.
  • Never leave cooking unattended; keep cloth away from flames.
  • Store LPG upright, away from heat, with the valve closed when not in use.
  • Check electrical wiring and avoid overloading extension boards.
  • Plan two exits from every room and practise them at night.
  • If a fire starts: alert everyone, get out, stay out, call 101/112. Do not re-enter.

Fire as a tool

  • Boiling water: rolling boil for 1 minute.
  • Cooking: coals give steadier heat than flames; a bed of embers is ideal for rice and stews.
  • Warmth: reflect heat with a wall of logs or a space blanket behind you.
  • Signalling: three fires in a triangle is a distress signal; at night, three evenly spaced fires.
  • Drying: dry clothing and tinder near — never over — the fire.
  • Repellent: smoke reduces mosquitoes; do not rely on it as your only protection.
