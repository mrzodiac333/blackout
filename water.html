Water: Find, Treat, Store

How to find water, make it safe to drink, and store enough for your household — the single highest-priority survival skill.

Survival Last reviewed 2026-10-19 10 min read

A person needs roughly 3 litres of drinking water per day; cooking and basic hygiene push household use to 10–20 litres per person per day. You can survive weeks without food but only days without water. Treat water as your first supply problem.

Assume all untreated water is contaminated

Flood water, river water, roof runoff and open wells can carry bacteria, viruses, protozoa and chemicals. Clear water is not safe water. Always treat before drinking.

For a family of four, two weeks at 10 L/day/person is 560 litres — about 28 standard 20-litre cans. That is a lot of space. Most households cannot store it all, so pair storage with treatment capability.

Finding water

  • Your own tank and geyser. Overhead tanks, sumps and even a water heater hold usable water. Shut off incoming mains first if supply is contaminated.
  • Rainwater. Collect from a clean roof into covered containers; first flush is dirtiest.
  • Wells and borewells. Usually reliable but vulnerable to flood contamination.
  • Rivers, streams and lakes. Prefer moving water upstream of settlements and livestock.
  • Groundwater. A seep dug downslope in a depression, filtered through soil, may collect clearer water. Still treat it.
Do not drink

Flood water, water with a chemical smell, water near industrial sites or fuel spills, and seawater. Chemical and salt contamination cannot be removed by boiling or household filters.

Treating water

1. Boiling (most reliable)

Bring water to a rolling boil for at least 1 minute (3 minutes above 2,000 m). Boiling kills bacteria, viruses and protozoa. It does not remove chemicals or make muddy water clear. Filter cloudy water through cloth first, and let boiled water cool covered.

2. Chlorination

Use unscented household bleach (sodium hypochlorite) or chlorine tablets. Follow the product label; generally 2 drops of 5% bleach per litre of clear water, wait 30 minutes. Cloudy water needs double the dose or pre-filtering. If it does not smell faintly of chlorine after 30 minutes, re-dose. Do not use bleach with additives or fragrance.

3. Filters

Commercial hollow-fibre or ceramic filters remove bacteria and protozoa but usually not viruses. A DIY sand-and-charcoal filter improves clarity and removes some chemicals but is not a disinfectant — always disinfect afterwards.

4. Solar disinfection (SODIS)

Fill clear PET bottles with filtered water, lay them in strong sun for at least 6 hours (2 days if cloudy). Effective against viruses and bacteria; less so in turbid water.

Storing water

  • Use food-grade containers with tight lids; keep them out of sunlight.
  • Label with the fill date. Commercially sealed water lasts about two years; home-filled water should be rotated every 6 months.
  • Store in a cool place away from fuel, paint and cleaning chemicals.
  • Do not store water in old milk jugs or chemical drums — they are hard to clean and can leach.
  • Freeze water bottles in the freezer for both storage and fridge backup.
  • Keep at least one portable container per person for evacuation.
A note on bathtubsFilling a clean bathtub before a known emergency gives you flushing and washing water, not drinking water unless treated.

Heat, dehydration and water discipline

  • Drink before you feel thirsty; thirst is a late sign.
  • Urine should be pale yellow. Dark urine means drink more.
  • In extreme heat, rest during midday, wear loose light clothing, and add salt to food.
  • Never ration water to the point of dehydration. Ration activity instead.
  • Treat dehydration with oral rehydration salts (ORS), not just plain water.
